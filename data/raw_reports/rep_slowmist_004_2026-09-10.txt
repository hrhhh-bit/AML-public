On September 9, the U.S. Department of the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) announced coordinated enforcement actions against Xinbi Guarantee. OFAC designated Xinbi Guarantee and two companies that provided operational support to the platform, while the DOJ’s Scam Center Strike Force seized related platform infrastructure and cryptocurrency wallets. According to the DOJ announcement, the coordinated action restricted more than $52 million in crypto assets linked to Xinbi Guarantee and its merchant network.
The action targeted not a single scam group, but the service network that supports the operation of scam activities. U.S. law enforcement agencies stated that Xinbi Guarantee connected scam groups with merchants providing services such as money laundering and scam website development, and provided the conditions for these illicit transactions to operate continuously through fund custody and transaction guarantees.
OFAC designated Xinbi Guarantee as a significant transnational criminal organization pursuant to Executive Order 13581, as amended. Also sanctioned were Singapore-based SafeW Technology and Cambodia-based Anwen Technology. The U.S. Department of the Treasury stated that the former developed the SafeW communications application used by buyers and sellers to communicate on Xinbi Guarantee, while the latter developed XinbiPay (also known as NewPay wallet), a crypto payment and digital wallet application.
The DOJ’s actions focused directly on the platform’s channels and funds. On September 7, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia authorized the seizure of Telegram channels used to operate the marketplace. Law enforcement subsequently seized two wallets used by the platform to collect payments on behalf of merchants, which held approximately $12 million in total, and sought to restrict assets in another 47 wallets believed to be associated with Xinbi Guarantee’s money laundering network and related merchants. The DOJ stated that the coordinated action restricted more than $52 million in crypto assets and, in its announcement, thanked Tether (the issuer of USDT) for its assistance.
Xinbi Guarantee began operating around 2022 and is a Chinese-language online guarantee marketplace. According to data disclosed by the U.S. Department of the Treasury, the marketplace and related platforms have processed more than $24 billion worth of digital asset and fiat currency transactions since their establishment. This figure reflects the transaction volume processed by the platforms and does not represent verified scam losses or the amount of assets seized.
In terms of how it operates, the Xinbi Guarantee platform combines merchant listings, fund custody, and transaction guarantees. Merchants publish service information through channels such as Telegram, where scam groups look for money laundering services, customized scam websites, and other resources for carrying out illicit activities. According to disclosures from the DOJ, after a buyer purchases a service, the platform may temporarily hold the payment that should be paid to the merchant and release it after the service is completed. According to announcements from Xinbi Guarantee, merchants are required to pay a security deposit, and if a buyer is deceived by a merchant, they may receive compensation through the relevant mechanism. Historically, merchants primarily used USDT on the TRON blockchain to pay the security deposit.
Within the network formed by these merchants, the sources of funds may also vary. According to matching against SlowMist’s threat intelligence database, some money laundering merchants known as “black U” merchants received stolen crypto assets that could be traced back to hacking incidents, and then delivered stablecoins originating from other illicit revenue streams to their customers, including proceeds from pig-butchering scams and romance scams. This indicates that different types of illicit funds may converge within the same merchant network and be transferred and exchanged through different addresses and forms of assets.
Therefore, analyzing underground marketplaces of this kind requires more than simply focusing on the platform itself; it is also necessary to identify the merchant network behind the platform and its receiving addresses. The platform facilitates transactions through advertising, fund custody, and deposit mechanisms, while specific money laundering activities may be carried out by different merchants. If investigations or risk controls focus only on the platform name or monitor only a small number of publicly known wallets, they may miss the receiving addresses actually used by merchants, as well as the subsequent flow of funds from those addresses.
The development of Xinbi Guarantee is also related to the migration of business activities following regulatory and law enforcement actions against similar marketplaces. The U.S. Department of the Treasury stated that after the Huione ecosystem was affected by regulatory measures, some cybercriminal activities shifted to Xinbi Guarantee, which continued to provide similar services to an overlapping customer base. The Treasury also disclosed that, starting around June 2025, Xinbi Guarantee began moving its merchants and money laundering networks to SafeW, while launching XinbiPay (also known as NewPay wallet) during the same period.
