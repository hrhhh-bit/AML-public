On September 4, 2026, the well-known decentralized lending platform Notional Finance was attacked, resulting in approximately $1.73 million in losses. The following is a detailed analysis of this attack by the SlowMist Security Team:
In Notional Finance V1, fCash can be understood as cash claims with an expiration date. The cash receiver receives payment at maturity, while the cash payer makes the payment at maturity. The protocol records both types of positions in the Portfolio, and then determines whether an account is healthy enough to establish a position through a free collateral check.
The safeTransferFrom of ERC1155Trade serves as an asset accounting function here. When the transferred asset type is a cash receiver, the contract calls Portfolios.mintfCashPair, recording a liability for the payer and an equivalent claim for the receiver. Although this call looks like an ERC1155 transfer, it actually results in the paired minting of fCash.
After a new position is written to the Portfolio, the free collateral must be calculated and checked. The RiskFramework contract first aggregates the payer’s fCash liabilities into a signed int256, and then converts the balances of each currency into ETH for aggregation. A negative value represents cash that needs to be paid, while a positive value represents cash or claims owned by the account. This calculation determines whether the transaction can be allowed to proceed.
After the position matures, the Portfolio calls the Escrow’s portfolioSettleCash function to convert the matured fCash into a cash balance. The account then withdraws the corresponding DAI or USDC assets from the Escrow through the withdrawal function.
The core vulnerability behind this attack lies in ExchangeRate._convertToETH used by the Escrow implementation contract, where a signed integer’s absolute value is directly converted to uint128.
uint128 absBalance = uint128(balance.abs());
// If balance.abs() exceeds uint128, the conversion result will be silently truncated
int256 result = int256(
SafeCast.toUint128(rate.mul(absBalance)... /* subsequent conversion */)
);
return balance > 0 ? result : result.neg();
The attacker first established two liability positions with the same payer, with amounts of 1 and uint128.max, respectively. Adding the two liabilities together resulted in exactly 2¹²⁸. Since payments by the payer are recorded as negative values in the risk calculation, the key parameter passed to Escrow’s convertBalancesToETH was -2¹²⁸.
However, the Solidity version used by this contract is 0.6.x, so its absolute value, 2¹²⁸, would overflow to 0 after being forcibly converted to uint128, meaning that this liability was not included in the ETH-denominated result.
Finally, the mintfCashPair function only requires the payer’s free collateral to be greater than or equal to zero. Therefore, after the result overflows to 0, the check can be passed, allowing an account without sufficient assets to cover its liabilities to still establish a position.
1. In the preceding transaction (0xe1589a19…d60a), the attacker first created multiple auxiliary contracts and called the setApprovalForAl function of the ERC1155Trade contract to authorize the auxiliary contracts. In addition, the attacker queried the maturity dates of two CashMarkets in advance and calculated the values of the three AssetId parameters that needed to be passed in the subsequent operations.
2. Immediately afterward, the attacker called the safeTransferFrom function of the ERC1155Trade contract to mint an fCash pair with an amount of 1. The corresponding bond asset had a cashGroupId of 2 and a maturity timestamp of 1788480000 (September 4, 2026, at 08:00). This called the internal function _upsertAsset, which updated the liability and expected income of the from address (the attack contract) and the to address (receiving contract 1), respectively.
After the pair was minted, Portfolios immediately checked the free collateral of the attack contract. Since the amount of the first mint was too small, its conversion into ETH under the current exchange rate and precision was rounded down to zero, allowing the first check to pass.
3. The attacker then called the safeTransferFrom function of the ERC1155Trade contract again to mint an fCash pair with an amount of uint128.max. This time, the corresponding bond asset also had a cashGroupId of 2, but its maturity timestamp was 1796256000 (December 3, 2026, at 08:00).
There is a detail here: the attacker minted bond assets with different maturity dates to two different addresses, respectively. This is because when updating the liability of the from address, if the bond assets were the same, they would be directly added together, causing an overflow during the addition and resulting in a revert (the contract uses the SafeMath library).
4. After the second mint updated the liability and expected income of the from address (the attack contract) and the to address (receiving contract 2), the mintfCashPair function called the freeCollateral function to calculate the net collateral position of the from address and check that it was greater than or equal to 0 for the position to be considered healthy.

The final result is -2¹²⁸. It then calls the convertBalancesToETH function of the Escrow contract to convert this value into an ETH-denominated value, while the convertBalancesToETH function in turn calls the _convertToETH function of the ExchangeRate library for the calculation.
Following the _convertToETH function, it can be found that it first takes the absolute value of balance and then calls uint128 to convert the 256-bit value to 128 bits. Since the total liability of the from address above is -2¹²⁸, taking its absolute value gives 2¹²⁸, and when uint128() is used for the forced conversion, the value exceeds the upper limit and overflows to 0. This means that the protocol incorrectly considers the free collateral position of the from address to be 0 and healthy, thereby passing the final check in the mintfCashPair function.
5. The receiving contract 2 then split its position among two other auxiliary contracts. These two safeTransferFrom calls still entered the mintfCashPair function. However, because the payer at this point was receiving contract 2 from the second mint, which held the huge expected income position obtained in the previous step, the risk calculation treated it as a positive claim and therefore passed the collateral check. The two auxiliary contracts thus obtained receiver fCash that could be converted into cash upon maturity.
6. The attacker then initiated the second formal profit transaction. In this transaction, the corresponding claims of the two auxiliary contracts holding the positions from the previous transaction were settled, increasing the cash balance of the corresponding assets. The attacker then called the withdraw function of the Escrow contract to withdraw the assets and realize the profit.
The key point of this attack is that the attacker first used two separate positions to make the sum of the payer’s liabilities exactly 2¹²⁸, and then had the Escrow convert this liability into ETH. By exploiting the overflow vulnerability in the type conversion to truncate the result to zero, the risk check was therefore allowed to pass.
The SlowMist Security Team recommends that projects must perform range checks before type conversions, and directly reject results exceeding type(uint128).max. At the same time, all boundaries involving signed amounts, precision scaling, and asset denominations should be covered by extreme-value testing, especially inputs such as uint128.max, uint128.max + 1, and the absolute values of negative numbers. The SafeCast library from OpenZeppelin can be referenced or used for conversion handling.
