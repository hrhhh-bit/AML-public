Blockchain security happens at the intersection of code, infrastructure, and regulation. That's why we're glad to share that CertiK has joined LF Decentralized Trust (LFDT), the Linux Foundation's community for building open-source, enterprise-grade decentralized technologies. We're joining as part of a cohort of new members announced ahead of Sibos, adding our name to the growing momentum behind open, standards-based infrastructure for decentralized systems.
The Regulatory Ground Has Shifted
Our own research, most recently the Skynet State of Digital Asset Regulations Report, has tracked this shift across major markets including the US, EU, Hong Kong, Singapore, the UAE, Japan, Turkey, and Brazil. Independent smart contract audits are now mandated or indirectly required for licensing and token admission in most jurisdictions. AML enforcement has overtaken securities classification as the industry's biggest regulatory headache, with related fines and settlements topping $900 million in H1 2025 alone. And exchanges, custodians, and issuers are increasingly held to the same prudential bar as traditional financial institutions — capital adequacy, asset segregation, liquidity management, all of it. The takeaway for anyone building enterprise blockchain infrastructure is that security and compliance are table stakes.
Why LFDT
LFDT brings together enterprises, financial institutions, and technology providers to build the open-source infrastructure that decentralized systems increasingly run on — spanning finance, banking, supply chain, healthcare, and telecom. Its model is deliberately vendor-neutral and standards-driven, and that's exactly where security expertise does the most good: not locked inside one company's stack, but built into the shared infrastructure the whole industry runs on.
What CertiK Brings to the Table
Since 2017, we've secured more than $600 billion in digital assets across more than 5,000 enterprise engagements, and our research — spanning digital asset regulation, DeFi exploits, and nation-state threat actors — has become a widely cited reference point on where security and compliance risk is heading next. As an LFDT member, we're bringing that experience straight into the community: contributing to open-source projects and working groups, and showing up where the conversation is happening.
